# The November scorecard is up

Subject: Spokane scorecard, November: still off track, and one flag we planted paid off

The second edition of the Spokane Prosperity Scorecard is live. The verdict
is unchanged: off track. One measure strong, three mixed, four weak. But a
lot moved underneath, and one thing this page predicted came true.

Here is the quarter in five lines.

**Jobs are still shrinking, more slowly.** The county had 1,400 fewer jobs
in September than a year earlier. In June that gap was 2,900. The metro has
lost an average of 133 jobs a month in 2026, the first losing year since 2020.

**Incomes stood still while the country moved.** Income per person rose $10
in 2025. Ten dollars. The nation grew 4.2%. That dropped Spokane from 97% of
the U.S. figure to 94%, and the grade from mixed to weak.

**Remember last year's household income headline?** The 2024 estimate said
Spokane's median household income beat the nation's for the first time. This
page flagged it as provisional, because single-year survey jumps earn
skepticism. The 2025 estimate came in: $81,175, back below the U.S. The flag
was right. It is all in the change log.

**Storefronts are the good news.** Retail vacancy fell to 4.7%, the biggest
one-quarter drop since 2021, after the strongest quarter of filled space in
at least two years. Nordstrom Rack, World Market and Trader Joe's took boxes
that dead chains left behind. If this holds, a grade goes green, and the
page will say so in green, with credit.

**The unemployment card retired.** As announced in August, it was replaced
by a harder question: what share of adults 20 to 64 hold a job. Answer:
75.9%, level with the nation. Not bad, not an edge. Mixed.

One more thing. The grading rules are now numbers, published in the
methodology before the re-grade, so you can check every call. That was the
promise for November, and it is kept.
